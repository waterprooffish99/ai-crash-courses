## 🧠 Part 1 · The Big Picture

The course begins with a simple idea: having access to a powerful AI does not automatically make you good at using it.

**Imagine this:** You hire a talented new employee and say only, “Make a course about AI agents.” The employee makes something polished, but it is for PhD researchers, too long, and has no practice. The employee was capable. **Your instruction was incomplete.** AI has the same problem, except it may also confidently invent facts.

### AI access ≠ AI fluency

#### ✅ Effective

**Meaning:** You reach the real goal.

**Example:** The study plan actually helps you prepare for the exam.

#### ⚡ Efficient

**Meaning:** You do not waste time, effort, or tokens.

**Tokens:** small pieces of text an AI reads and writes.

#### ⚖️ Ethical

**Meaning:** You use AI fairly and honestly.

**Example:** You do not mislead people about AI's role when that role matters.

#### 🛡️ Safe

**Meaning:** You protect people, privacy, security, and important information.

**Example:** Do not paste private customer data into an unapproved tool.

**🎯 Exam point:** AI fluency has four qualities: **effective, efficient, ethical, safe.**

### The 4Ds

**Delegation**DECIDE  
What should AI do?

**Description**EXPLAIN  
What does AI need?

**Discernment**CHECK  
Is the result good?

**Diligence**OWN  
Is this responsible?

### Three facts you must carry from “What AI Actually Is”

1.  **Sounding correct is not the same as being correct.** AI can confidently produce wrong information.
2.  **The same prompt may not always give exactly the same answer.** AI output can vary.
3.  **AI can only use information available to it.** That may include training, your chat, files, memory, web search, or connected tools. Missing information can lead to guessing.

**Easy picture:** Treat AI like a very capable colleague whose work is useful, fast, and still needs review. Unlike a careful human expert, AI may sound equally confident when it is right and when it is wrong.

| Course | Main job |

| **AI Fluency** | Learn how a human should work with that machine. |

The tools and prompting tricks will change over time. The 4D competencies are meant to remain useful even when the models change.

## 🤝 Three Ways to Work with AI

These modes mainly differ by autonomy, which means **how much freedom the AI has to decide its next step.**

### 1\. Automation

**Meaning:** “Do this defined task.”

You provide the task or steps. AI has little freedom.

**Examples:** summarize a report, translate an email, extract invoice fields.

**You are like:** a script writer.

### 2\. Augmentation

**Meaning:** “Help me think.”

You and AI work back and forth as thinking partners.

**Examples:** brainstorm an idea, compare strategies, review an architecture.

**You are like:** a co-creator.

### 3\. Agency

**Meaning:** “Pursue this goal for me.”

You provide a goal and boundaries. AI chooses many steps.

**Example:** keep an inbox manageable, reply to routine messages, flag important ones, ask before uncertain actions.

**You are like:** a director.

### Automation vs Agency

|  | Automation | Agency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You provide | Task / steps | Goal / boundaries |
| AI decides | Very little | Many next steps |
| Main failure | A step is done badly | The goal or boundary is misunderstood |
| Your role | Script writer | Director |

**Important:** Agency is harder because you may not be watching. “Future” means the AI can act later when you are absent. “For others” means it may serve customers, students, or colleagues, not only you. Therefore the judgment and safety rules must be designed beforehand.

No mode is always “better.” One project can use all three: automate extraction, use augmentation for difficult exceptions, then give an agent limited authority over routine cases.

### Agent Factory connection

**Mode 1** mainly uses automation and augmentation to solve problems. **Mode 2** makes agency systematic by building Digital FTEs.

Digital FTE = an AI worker set up to do a defined job for other people. **FTE** means “full-time equivalent,” a business term for one full-time worker.

A Digital FTE is not simply “AI doing things.” It needs a defined job, permissions, rules, governance, and reliable business information.

System of Record (SoR) = the trusted place where a business keeps its official data and rules.

## 1️⃣ Delegation · Decide Who Should Do What

Delegation means deciding how the work should be divided between the human and AI. It is workflow design, not just dumping tasks on AI.

### Problem Awareness

Know the goal, work, risks, audience, success criteria, and where human judgment is essential.

### Platform Awareness

Know what kind of AI system or tool fits the job.

### Task Delegation

Decide which part belongs to AI, which belongs to a human, and which needs both.

### 3.1 Problem awareness

Before prompting, ask:

-   What is the actual goal?
-   Who is this for?
-   What does success look like?
-   What could go wrong?
-   Which decisions need human judgment?

**Example:** “Build an invoice-chasing agent” is not enough. You must first decide which customers it may contact, how late an invoice must be, what tone to use, when human approval is required, what happens during disputes, what systems it may read, and whether it may send messages or only draft them. These are **business-policy questions**, not just prompting questions.

### 3.2 Platform awareness

Different AI systems fit different jobs:

-   **Reasoning model:** useful for difficult multi-step thinking.
-   **Search-enabled assistant:** useful when current web information matters.
-   **Coding agent:** useful for reading and changing software project files.
-   **Agent-capable system:** useful for multi-step work where tools are used and the system continues without a new instruction every step.

You do not need to memorize model brand names. Models change quickly. Build the habit: **“Is this the right tool for this job?”**

### 3.3 Task delegation

| Course-creation task | Best owner | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choose audience and learning goals | Human | Purpose and judgment |
| Suggest possible structures | AI + Human | AI gives options; human chooses |
| Draft from an agreed outline | AI | Fast first drafts |
| Verify factual claims | Human | Accountability remains human |
| Add lived experience / local examples | Human | AI does not have your life experience |
| Improve grammar and consistency | AI | Good mechanical task |
| Approve the final work | Human | Your name and reputation are attached |

**🎯 Better question:** Do not ask only, “Can AI do this?” Ask: **“Which parts should AI do, which parts should I do, and why?”**

### When Delegation becomes engineering

For a Digital FTE, problem awareness becomes a specification: the goal, constraints, risks, and definition of done. Task delegation becomes the worker's **boundary**: what AI may do, what humans keep, and what must escalate.

**Escalate** = stop and pass the case to a human or higher authority because it is risky, unusual, or outside the AI's permission.

## 2️⃣ Description · Give AI What It Needs

Description means giving AI enough information and guidance to do the work well. If important information is missing, AI may make a reasonable-looking guess that is still wrong.

Memory aid: **WHAT → HOW → HOW TO WORK WITH ME / USERS**

### Product Description

**WHAT** do you want back?

### Process Description

**HOW** should the work be approached?

### Performance Description

**HOW SHOULD AI BEHAVE** with you or future users?

### 4.1 Product description: define the result

Specify the output type, audience, format, length, tone, important topics, and exclusions.

**Weak:** “Summarize this report.”  
  
**Better:** “Summarize this quarterly report for senior executives. Focus on revenue changes, major risks, and actions. Use short bullets, keep it to one page, highlight big changes from last quarter, and avoid unnecessary accounting jargon.”

**Main lesson:** the second request is not “smarter.” It is more complete.

### 4.2 Process description: define the approach

You can define steps, order, methods, examples to imitate, and checks before finishing.

**Example:** For code review, tell AI to check **correctness first, security second, style last.** This prevents it from wasting attention on naming before confirming that the code even works.

#### Why multi-step work should often be split

If AI extracts facts, compares vendors, scores them, and writes a recommendation in one giant step, a wrong fact at the start can silently infect everything after it.

1.  **Extract:** Put the same facts from each proposal into one table. Verify sample cells.
2.  **Compare:** Compare only from that verified table.
3.  **Score:** Apply the criteria you actually gave. Check that AI did not invent new criteria.
4.  **Recommend:** Write the recommendation only from the checked earlier steps.

**🎯 Key principle:** Put the step where an error could spread the farthest **early**, then check it before continuing.

### 4.3 Performance description: define behavior

This tells AI how to act, not merely what document to produce.

-   concise or detailed
-   supportive or challenging
-   exploratory or decisive
-   ask questions first or make reasonable assumptions
-   flag uncertainty or simply give a best answer

**Example:** “Challenge weak assumptions. Flag uncertainty. Do not agree just to be polite. Change your recommendation when new evidence supports a change, and explain why you disagree.”

At chat scale, this shapes how AI works with **you**. At agent scale, it becomes standing behavior for many users. Example: a tutoring agent may be instructed never to reveal the answer before the student tries.

**Important:** In a chat, bad performance description is annoying. In a deployed agent, its behavior **is part of the product.**

### Prompt Engineering vs Context Engineering

| Term | Super simple meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Prompt Engineering** | How should I phrase this instruction? |
| **Context Engineering** | What information must be available so AI can succeed? |

Context engineering can include instructions, documents, examples, memory, conversation history, policies, tools, database records, definitions, and rules.

**Easy analogy:** A prompt is the instruction you give a chef. Context is the entire kitchen: recipe, ingredients, tools, dietary rules, examples, and customer preferences. A beautiful instruction cannot fix missing ingredients.

### How Description scales in Agent Factory

-   System prompt: a standing instruction read at the start, useful for permanent behavior rules.
-   `SKILL.md`: can make a process reusable.
-   **System of Record:** holds trusted domain knowledge, rules, and governance.

## 3️⃣ Discernment · Do Not Confuse Confidence with Correctness

Discernment means judging whether AI's output, support, and behavior are good enough.

**Core danger:** AI can sound polished, detailed, and completely certain while being wrong.

### Automation bias

Automation bias = the human tendency to trust automated output too easily, especially when it looks professional.

**Example:** AI gives a precise percentage with a professional citation. Your brain thinks, “It looks researched.” Discernment asks, “Does that source really exist, and does it actually say this?”

### Four warning signs of a made-up answer

1.  **Suspiciously exact details:** precise figures, dates, names, or citations that appeared from nowhere.
2.  **Too much certainty:** an expert would say “it depends,” but AI gives a flat yes/no.
3.  **Internal contradictions:** later parts disagree with earlier parts.
4.  **Claims that an action happened without evidence:** “I sent it,” “I opened the source,” or “I ran the tests” when no sent message, opened page, or test log exists.

**🎯 Rule:** When accuracy matters, **verify.**

### Product Discernment

Is the actual result good?

### Process Discernment

Is this way of working with AI actually saving effort?

### Performance Discernment

When AI acts independently, does its behavior serve users well?

### 5.1 Product discernment

Ask:

-   Is it factually correct?
-   Did it follow important requirements?
-   Is anything missing?
-   Is it internally consistent?
-   Would an expert find it credible?
-   **Would I put my name on it?**

Check the result against three things: **your request, the source material, and the standards of the field.**

Your own domain knowledge matters. An accountant catches accounting mistakes. A programmer sees bugs. A teacher sees explanations that will confuse beginners.

**Domain knowledge** = real knowledge and experience in a particular field.

#### Check the justification, not just the conclusion

A recommendation can accidentally be correct even if the assumptions behind it are wrong. Ask AI to show **assumptions, evidence, criteria, calculations, intermediate results, and alternative interpretations**.

**Important technical point:** Treat that as a justification you can review, **not** as a literal transcript of the model's hidden internal reasoning.

#### Ground answers in supplied documents

When the answer must come from documents, tell AI to use only those documents, say when information is missing instead of guessing, and point you to the relevant section or evidence so you can verify quickly.

### 5.2 Process discernment

Judge the whole working session:

-   Is AI adapting to my corrections?
-   Does it repeat the same mistake?
-   Has it become so agreeable that it is useless?
-   Am I repeatedly fixing the same formatting problem?
-   Am I editing more than I would have written myself?

**Simple math:** 20 minutes of steering that saves 60 minutes is useful. 20 minutes of steering that saves only 15 minutes is not efficient, even if the session felt productive.

When the process is bad, escalate through three moves: **change the performance description → change the tool → take the task back.** Taking work back is also AI fluency.

### 5.3 Performance discernment

This matters when you use **agency**. A system can produce correct individual answers and still behave badly across users.

**Example:** An AI tutor may answer every math question correctly but still be a bad tutor if it immediately gives answers whenever a student hesitates. Correct output, poor teaching behavior.

You cannot judge system behavior from one chat. You look across many cases: complaints, outcomes, repeated failures, and user behavior.

### The Description ↔ Discernment loop

**Describe**  
Say what you need

→

**AI produces**  
Get a draft

→

**Discern**  
Inspect it

→

**Refine**  
Explain the change

The first answer is usually a **draft**, not the finish line.

#### Best feedback pattern

Problem → Why it matters → Direction

**Weak:** “Wrong. Try again.”  
  
**Better:** “This section assumes large-company customers. Our readers are solo founders, so the advice is too expensive. Rewrite it for a one-person business with a limited budget.”

#### Use named editing passes

-   **Clarity pass:** Does each sentence clearly say one thing?
-   **Tone pass:** Does the writing fit this audience?
-   **Formatting pass:** Are headings, bullets, and length correct?

For important work, you can ask for two or three drafts and edit from the strongest.

#### Every review should end in one of three decisions

**Ready to use**

Ship it.

**Needs revision**

Send specific feedback.

**Needs human override**

Take the task back.

### How Discernment scales into engineering

Manual checking becomes evaluation engineering.

-   **Eval:** a repeatable test that scores an AI output.
-   **Eval suite:** a collection of such tests.
-   **Monitoring:** watching system behavior over time.
-   **Sampling:** reviewing selected examples from many outputs.
-   **Release / review gates:** checks that must pass before a system or output can move forward.

**Key idea:** You cannot automate a judgment you never learned to make manually.

## 4️⃣ Diligence · Use AI Responsibly and Own the Result

Diligence asks a different question from the first three Ds: **“Should I use AI this way at all?”**

**Example:** A lecturer gets excellent AI-written student feedback, but pasted student names, grades, and disciplinary notes into an unapproved consumer AI tool. The output may be excellent, but the method is still irresponsible.

### Creation Diligence

Choose tools, data, and context responsibly before and during the work.

### Transparency Diligence

Be honest about AI's role when that role matters to people.

### Deployment Diligence

Check and take responsibility before work is sent, used, executed, or published.

### 6.1 Creation diligence

Before sharing information with AI, ask:

-   Does this contain personal data?
-   Does it contain confidential company information?
-   Am I allowed to put it in this tool?
-   Who can access or retain the data?
-   Is this tool approved?
-   Are there legal, contractual, or professional restrictions?

#### Redaction

Redaction = removing identifying details before giving data to AI, while keeping enough of the useful pattern for the task.

**Example:** AI may need “student scored 62% and repeatedly missed deadlines,” but not the student's name, ID, address, or other details that identify the person.

**Two redaction failures:** Remove too much and AI cannot do the task. Remove too little and a combination of remaining details can still identify the person. Also, redaction does not automatically make an unapproved tool acceptable.

### 6.2 Transparency diligence

Not every tiny AI-assisted task requires an announcement. But disclosure becomes more important when AI materially affects other people.

Examples include academic work, hiring, customer communications, medical or financial advice, professional reports, and work represented as purely human-made.

**Rule of thumb:** The more an AI-assisted result affects other people, the stronger the case for transparency.

The exact rule can depend on law, organization policy, professional standards, and context.

### 6.3 Deployment diligence

Before work leaves your hands, check it. More impact means more checking. Irreversible actions deserve checks **before** execution.

| Example | Level of checking |
| --- | --- |
| Private note to yourself | Quick glance |
| Customer-facing email | Full read |
| Report to regulator / high-stakes body | Deeper review, possibly a second reviewer |
| Sent payment / deleting files | Pre-action approval because it may be hard or impossible to undo |

Checking can include facts, sources, calculations, bias, permissions, rights, policy compliance, and human approval for high-impact actions.

### 🔢 The Numbers Rule

**Any number that a decision depends on should be computed, not merely generated by a language model.**

A language model predicts plausible text. It does not automatically behave like a calculator or spreadsheet. Use a calculator, spreadsheet, or executed code, then inspect the inputs used in the calculation.

**Example:** If AI summarizes a financial table and says total revenue is 8.7 million, do not trust the total just because the row values look correct. Make the number come from computation, then verify that the right rows and rates were used.

### The final Diligence question

“Would I confidently put my name on this?”  
If the answer is no, it is not ready.

### When the ethical case is unclear

1.  Who is affected, including people who never see the result?
2.  What could go wrong for them, and would they know?
3.  What would a fair outcome look like?
4.  What should be disclosed, and to whom?

If you cannot responsibly answer these, escalate to whoever owns the decision rather than guessing.

**🎯 Must remember:** AI can automate work. **It cannot automate accountability.** Humans and organizations still own the consequences.

### How Diligence scales in Agent Factory

| Personal skill | System-scale version |
| --- | --- |
| Creation diligence | Data rules, access controls, approved-tool policy |
| Transparency diligence | Disclosure and user-experience design |
| Deployment diligence | Evaluation gates, audit logs, monitoring, human review |

Audit log = a record of what the system did, when, and often who or what triggered it. It helps with accountability and investigation.

### Complete example: Bookkeeping Digital FTE

A Forward Deployed Engineer helps an accounting practice automate monthly bank reconciliation, meaning matching the firm's own transaction records against the bank statement.

<Exercise title={"Step 1 · Delegation"}>

The team decides that the AI may match bank transactions, flag unmatched items, and draft a reconciliation report. Humans still approve journal adjustments, write-offs, and tax-sensitive decisions. High-value unusual items must escalate.

**Terms:** *ledger entry* = the firm's record of a transaction. *journal adjustment* = a manual correction in the accounts. *write-off* = recording money the firm no longer expects to collect. *tax position* = how tax rules apply to the client's situation.

</Exercise>

<Exercise title={"Step 2 · Description"}>

The AI gets the chart of accounts, matching rules, examples of past reconciliations, report format, escalation rules, definitions, and strict rules such as “never post a journal entry itself” and “never contact a client directly.”

**Terms:** *chart of accounts* = the categories used to organize transactions. *stale cheque* = a cheque too old for the bank to pay under applicable rules.

</Exercise>

<Exercise title={"Step 3 · Discernment"}>

The team tests the AI against past reconciliations already trusted by the accountants. They measure correct matches, incorrect matches, correct escalations, over-escalation, and whether quality changes over time. They also sample apparently successful cases, because silent failures may otherwise stay hidden.

</Exercise>

<Exercise title={"Step 4 · Diligence"}>

Client financial data stays inside approved infrastructure. Actions are logged. Clients are informed about AI assistance where required or appropriate. A human partner still signs the work and remains accountable.

</Exercise>

## 🏭 From Chat Skill to Agent Factory System

| Competency | In a normal chat | At Agent Factory scale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Delegation** | Decide what to ask AI to do | Scope the Digital FTE and define the human/AI boundary |
| **Description** | Give instructions and context | System prompts, reusable skills, context engineering, Systems of Record |
| **Discernment** | Review the answer | Evals, monitoring, sampling, checker reliability, release gates |
| **Diligence** | Protect data and own the result | Governance, permissions, audit, disclosure, human review |

**Big idea:** Agent Factory does not replace AI fluency. It takes the same four human skills and builds them into systems that can run at scale.

## 🧭 Connection to the Four Survival Skills & 10-80-10

The course connects the 4Ds to the broader survival skills: set direction, orchestrate AI, judge truth, and connect with people.

| Work stage | Main activity | Strongest 4Ds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 10% | Set direction | Delegation + Description |
| Middle 80% | Orchestrate AI and iterate | Description + Discernment repeatedly |
| Final 10% | Judge the truth before important work ships | Discernment |
| All 100% | Act responsibly | Diligence surrounds the whole workflow |

**Exam trap:** Diligence is **not** only the final step. It applies across the whole workflow.

## ⚠️ Four Common Beginner Mistakes

### 1\. Prompt before defining the problem

**Missing:** Delegation

**Fix:** define goal, audience, constraints, and human/AI split first.

### 2\. Treat first answer as final

**Missing:** Description ↔ Discernment loop

**Fix:** inspect, give specific feedback, iterate.

### 3\. Trust polished writing

**Missing:** Discernment

**Fix:** verify facts, assumptions, calculations, and sources.

### 4\. Think about privacy after trouble

**Missing:** Diligence

**Fix:** set data, disclosure, approval, and accountability rules beforehand.

## ✅ Beginner Checklist for Everyday AI Work

| Stage | Ask yourself |
| --- | --- |
| **Delegate** | What is the goal? What should AI do? What stays with me? |
| **Describe** | What output, context, method, and behavior does AI need? |
| **Discern** | How will I know the result is correct, complete, and useful? |
| **Be diligent** | Is the data safe? Does AI's role need disclosure? Who approves and owns the outcome? |

You do not need paperwork for every small request. The goal is to make these questions automatic in your mind.

## 🛠️ Practical Work · Six Course Exercises

The official course asks you to practice the ideas in a real AI assistant. The prompts below are rewritten in simple language but preserve the same learning goal.

<Exercise title={"Exercise 1 · Build a 4D plan for a real task"}>

Choose one real task you need to do this week.

```text
I need to do this: [describe your real task].

Before we start, guide me through the four Ds:
1. Delegation
2. Description
3. Discernment
4. Diligence

Ask me one useful question at a time. Skip anything that clearly does not apply. At the end, summarize our plan in a small table.
```

**Notice:** many important answers should come from you. AI can remind you what to consider, but your goals, boundaries, and accountability are human decisions.

</Exercise>

<Exercise title={"Exercise 2 · Practice Discernment on a topic you know well"}>

```text
Let's discuss [a topic I know well].

Treat me like a knowledgeable colleague. During our discussion I will watch for:
- places where you improve my thinking,
- places where I need to correct you,
- suggestions I reject because my real experience says otherwise.

Start by asking which part of the topic I want to discuss.
```

**Notice:** errors are easy to spot when you already have domain expertise.

</Exercise>

<Exercise title={"Exercise 3 · Feel what being a non-expert is like"}>

```text
Teach me the basics of [a topic I know almost nothing about].

Use beginner-friendly language and concrete examples. At the end, identify the factual claims that I should verify with reliable sources, and explain why each one deserves checking.
```

**Notice:** when you know nothing about a field, wrong information may not “feel” wrong. This is why deployed AI systems need evals rather than blind trust.

</Exercise>

<Exercise title={"Exercise 4 · Write a performance description"}>

```text
For this conversation:
- challenge weak assumptions,
- flag uncertainty on factual claims,
- do not agree with me only to be polite,
- ask a clarifying question when ambiguity would materially change the answer,
- update your recommendation when new evidence supports a change,
- explain your reasons when you disagree.
```

**Notice:** the working behavior changes quickly. In a deployed agent, such standing behavior belongs in persistent instructions such as a system prompt.

</Exercise>

<Exercise title={"Exercise 5 · Inspect the support before accepting a recommendation"}>

```text
Before recommending an option, show me:
1. the important assumptions,
2. the evidence supporting them,
3. the criteria you are using to compare the options,
4. the major uncertainties.

Then give the recommendation. I want a justification I can review, not only a conclusion.
```

**Notice:** look for an assumption you might have accepted without seeing it.

</Exercise>

<Exercise title={"Exercise 6 · Run a small project through the full 4D loop"}>

Choose a project that can be completed fairly quickly, such as a study plan, small presentation, proposal, or coding task.

```text
I want to complete this project using the 4D AI Fluency framework:
[describe the project].

First help me decide the human/AI division of work.
Before each AI-owned task, ask what product, process, and performance I want.
After each important output, pause so I can evaluate it.
At the end, run a diligence check covering facts, sensitive data, disclosure, approvals, and anything that needs verification before I use the result.
```

After finishing, ask yourself: **Which D required the most effort from me?** That is probably the skill you need to practice most.

</Exercise>

## 📚 Technical Terms · Super Simple Glossary

**AI Fluency**  
Using AI effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely.

**4Ds**  
Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence.

**Automation**  
AI performs a clearly defined task you give it.

**Augmentation**  
Human and AI think and work together.

**Agency**  
AI works toward a goal and chooses many steps within boundaries.

**Autonomy**  
How much freedom a system has to decide what to do next.

**Delegation**  
Deciding what AI does and what humans keep.

**Problem Awareness**  
Knowing the goal, work, risks, audience, and what success means.

**Platform Awareness**  
Knowing which kind of AI or tool fits the job.

**Task Delegation**  
Assigning each part to AI, a human, or both.

**Description**  
Giving AI the information and guidance needed for the work.

**Product Description**  
Defining the output you want.

**Process Description**  
Defining the approach or steps AI should use.

**Performance Description**  
Defining how AI should behave with users or on its own.

**Prompt Engineering**  
Designing the wording and structure of an instruction.

**Context Engineering**  
Designing all information AI needs, such as instructions, files, tools, memory, and policies.

**System Prompt**  
A standing instruction that shapes AI behavior at the start of a session.

**System of Record**  
The trusted official store of business data and rules.

**Discernment**  
Judging whether AI output, justification, and behavior are good enough.

**Product Discernment**  
Judging the final result itself.

**Process Discernment**  
Judging whether your way of working with AI is worth the effort.

**Performance Discernment**  
Judging whether an autonomous AI serves users well across many cases.

**Automation Bias**  
Trusting automated output too easily because it looks authoritative.

**Hallucination**  
AI produces invented or incorrect information that may sound convincing.

**Diligence**  
Taking responsibility for how AI is used and what happens because of its output.

**Creation Diligence**  
Choosing tools and data responsibly before and during work.

**Transparency Diligence**  
Being honest about AI's role when it materially affects people.

**Deployment Diligence**  
Checking work before it is sent, used, published, or executed.

**Redaction**  
Removing identifying details while keeping useful task information.

**Digital FTE**  
An AI worker configured to perform a defined job for others.

**FTE**  
Full-time equivalent, a business measure representing one full-time worker.

**Tokens**  
Small pieces of text AI reads and writes, often related to usage cost.

**Domain Knowledge**  
Real expertise in a specific subject or industry.

**Specification**  
A clear definition of goal, requirements, constraints, risks, and completion conditions.

**Escalation**  
Passing a case to a human when it is risky, uncertain, unusual, or outside permission.

**Eval**  
A repeatable test that scores an AI system's output or behavior.

**Eval Suite**  
A group of eval tests used together.

**Monitoring**  
Watching system behavior and quality over time.

**Sampling**  
Reviewing selected examples from a much larger set of outputs.

**Release Gate**  
A required check that must pass before something is released or used.

**Governance**  
Rules, roles, permissions, oversight, and accountability controlling how AI is used.

**Audit Log**  
A record of system actions used for accountability and investigation.

**Bank Reconciliation**  
Comparing internal transaction records with a bank statement to find matches and differences.

## 🎯 What You Must Remember for the Exam

1.  **AI Fluency** = effective + efficient + ethical + safe AI use.
2.  **3 modes:** Automation = do a task; Augmentation = think with me; Agency = pursue a goal.
3.  **Delegation:** problem awareness + platform awareness + task delegation.
4.  **Description:** product + process + performance.
5.  **Discernment:** product + process + performance.
6.  **Diligence:** creation + transparency + deployment.
7.  **Prompt engineering** is about the instruction. **Context engineering** is about the whole information environment.
8.  **Automation bias:** people trust automated answers too easily.
9.  **Hallucination:** AI can confidently invent or get information wrong.
10.  **Numbers rule:** decision-critical numbers should be computed, not merely generated.
11.  **4D loop:** Delegate → Describe → Discern → Be diligent → Repeat.
12.  **10-80-10:** Delegation/Description early; Description/Discernment during; Discernment at final check; Diligence across all 100%.
13.  **Scale-up:** Discernment becomes evals/monitoring; Diligence becomes governance/permissions/audit/human review.
14.  **Final question:** “Would I confidently put my name on this?”

🔥 Final memory formula: **DECIDE → DESCRIBE → CHECK → OWN**
