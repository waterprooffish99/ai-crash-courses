## 🧠 First, build this picture in your mind

Imagine AI is a very fast junior worker and **you are the supervisor**.

**Asking**

“What are good AI-agent courses?”

AI gives advice. You still research, compare, check and decide.

**Delegating**

“Find 3 current free AI-agent courses, compare them in a table, give sources and recommend one.”

AI performs a chunk of the job. You mainly supervise and check.

**ASKING → an answer.**   |   **DELEGATING → a result you can inspect.**

Delegation means giving responsibility for producing a result to someone or something else. It does **not** mean giving away your responsibility for judging the result.

## 🔄 The Delegation Loop

This is the rhythm of the whole course:

Define→Delegate→Observe→Intervene→Verify→Accept

| Technical word | Super simple meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Define | Say what finished result you want. | “I want 3 courses compared.” |
| Delegate | Give AI responsibility for producing it. | “Research and make the comparison.” |
| Observe | Let AI do routine work and watch the result. | Read the table instead of building it yourself. |
| Intervene | Step in when AI drifts. | “Only free beginner courses.” |
| Verify | Check claims that matter. | Open the course page and confirm the price. |
| Accept | Human decides whether the result is fit to use. | Use it, send it back, or reject it. |

**Core picture:** AI owns much of the middle work. You own the beginning and the end. You decide what the job is, and you decide whether the result is good enough.

## 📋 The Delegation Brief: OCCADV

A Delegation Brief is a small **work order** written before AI starts. It gives you something concrete to compare the final result against.

**O • Outcome**

What should exist when finished?

**C • Context**

What does AI need to know that it cannot know itself?

**C • Constraints**

What requirements and limits apply?

**A • Authority**

What may AI decide without asking you?

**D • Deliverable**

What usable form should the result take?

**V • Verification**

What will you check before accepting?

**Example brief**  
Outcome: Compare 3 beginner AI-agent courses and recommend one.  
Context: I am a beginner and can study 5 hours weekly.  
Constraints: Must be free to start and require no programming knowledge.  
Authority: AI may choose sources; it must clearly flag paid requirements.  
Deliverable: Short table + recommendation + links.  
Verification: Mark anything not confirmed as **unverified**.

Important: this is a thinking tool, not paperwork. Tiny jobs do not need six formal boxes. The six questions help you diagnose why a disappointing result went wrong.

## 🚀 The 11 Concepts

### 1 From asking AI to giving AI the job

Ask for a **thing to exist**, not only advice. “Tell me about courses” leaves the work with you. “Find, compare and recommend 3 courses in a table” transfers meaningful work to AI.

🎯 **Exam clue:** Length is not the difference. Responsibility for producing a checkable result is the difference.

### 2 Give AI the outcome, not every click

Outcome = the finished result. A long recipe can trap AI inside your weak plan. Usually say what success looks like and let AI choose the method. Specify steps only when a particular source, sequence or method really matters.

Instead of “search site A, then site B...” say “When this is done, I will have three current beginner courses compared.”

### 3 Write the Delegation Brief

Use **Outcome, Context, Constraints, Authority, Deliverable, Verification**. The two commonly forgotten ideas are **Authority** and **Verification**. Authority prevents silent decisions. Verification tells you in advance what matters enough to check.

### 4 Give AI something to research

AI may answer from old learned information or may search now. Prices, dates, links, availability and “does this still exist?” require current information. A current claim should have a source you can open, but even a source can be stale.

**Never forget:** A citation is not verification. Opening the citation, finding the supporting information and checking its date is verification.

### 5 Give AI the source

If the needed information is in your PDF, policy, syllabus, notice or message thread, provide it. Tell AI to stay inside that source. For a short source, paste it. For a long one, attach it.

Useful pattern: “Use only the supplied source. Support each answer from that source. If the source does not contain the answer, say **not in the source**.”

Source grounding means tying the answer to provided evidence instead of allowing AI to freely mix in remembered information.

Remove unnecessary names, phone numbers, account numbers and private information. Temporary/incognito chat reduces persistence in normal history/memory, but it is not a magic privacy eraser.

If a long chat becomes confused or contradicts earlier decisions, summarize the decisions and remaining work, then start a fresh chat using that summary as context.

### 6 Ask for a deliverable, not just an answer

Deliverable = a finished work product someone can use, save or hand on, such as a document, table, spreadsheet or file. Specify form, sections and length. When converting chat into a polished artifact, make sure warnings such as “unverified” do not disappear.

### 7 Decide how much of the job AI owns

Authority = what AI may decide or do without asking you. Use plan-first: ask AI to show its planned steps and wait for your approval before starting. Before the run is the cheapest time to correct a bad approach.

| Boundary | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Research, but do not purchase | Money is hard to reverse. |
| Draft, but do not send | Sending creates an external commitment. |
| Inspect, but do not delete | Deletion can destroy evidence. |
| Choose, but tell me when you choose | Silent decisions cannot be reviewed. |

Before AI touches a real connected system, ask: **What may it read? What may it do? Do I trust the information source? Am I allowed to give it this access?**

### 8 Give AI the job again

Repeated work teaches you what should be saved. If you keep retyping the same background, sources and rules, move stable context into a Project. Files in a Project are Knowledge. Reusable rules are Standing Instructions.

**Memory trick:** Rules that stay the same → instructions. Sources that stay the same → knowledge. One stream of ongoing work → Project. Today's request → chat.

If what repeats is the **method** rather than the context, that points toward a Skill, which a later course teaches. Repeated work may later also need a schedule, but a job that ran automatically is not automatically a job that worked.

### 9 Intervene when AI goes wrong

Intervene = step in and change the direction. Use the smallest correction that works.

| Problem | Move |
| --- | --- |
| Wrong question | **Stop** and restate the outcome. |
| One fact/line wrong | **Correct** the specific error. |
| Heading wrong way | **Redirect** the direction. |
| Your job definition was wrong | **Change the requirement**. |
| AI lacks enough capability | **Escalate** to stronger reasoning/model, or break the job into smaller checked steps. |
| Still repeatedly failing | **Take the job back**. |

After fixing a drift, write the lesson back into the brief so the same failure is less likely next time.

### 10 Verify before you accept

This is not “read carefully.” It is a method. See the dedicated section below.

### 11 Same job, different AI

A good brief describes the **job**, not one product's buttons. Run the unchanged brief in another capable assistant or another fresh run. Record whether it finished, respected authority, preserved uncertainty and produced the deliverable. This is evidence about **those runs**, not proof that one AI is universally better.

The job belongs to you. The model is replaceable.

## 🔍 Verification: ITCD → ACIR

Identify→Trace→Challenge→Decide

| Step | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Identify | Choose claims important enough to check. |
| Trace | Go to the source, find support, check date and source quality. |
| Challenge | Ask how it could be wrong, outdated, assumed or based on a weaker secondary source. |
| Decide | Make the human decision. |

### Four possible decisions

**Accept**

Evidence is good enough. Use it.

**Correct**

A specific fix is needed.

**Investigate**

Evidence is incomplete or conflicting.

**Reject**

Not reliable enough. Take it back or use a qualified human.

### Review thresholds: when checking becomes especially important

Threshold means a condition that triggers stronger review. The four are:

**Stakes**

How costly would a mistake be?

**Reversibility**

Can the action easily be undone?

**Audience**

Who will see or rely on it?

**Regulatory exposure**

Is the field/data regulated?

One crossed threshold can be enough to put the result on your must-check list.

**Human review must stay:** final client deliverables, financially/audit-important calculations, regulated/confidential/highly sensitive work, and public/legal communications with lasting consequences.

AI doing the work does not transfer accountability to AI.

## 🛠️ Need First, Then Tool

Do not learn random buttons first. Start with the job's need.

| Job needs... | Then use... |
| --- | --- |
| Current information | Fresh web research/search. |
| A source only you have | Paste or attach the source. |
| Private content | Remove private parts; use temporary/incognito mode where appropriate. |
| A finished work product | Ask for the document/table/file. |
| Method freedom with boundaries | Authority line + plan-first. |
| Same context repeatedly | Project + Knowledge + Standing Instructions. |
| More capability | Stronger reasoning/model or smaller checked steps. |
| Information/action in another app | Connector, when appropriate. |

## 📚 Technical Terms You Must Know

**Delegation Loop**

Six-step rhythm: Define, Delegate, Observe, Intervene, Verify, Accept.

**Delegation Brief**

Six pre-work questions: Outcome, Context, Constraints, Authority, Deliverable, Verification.

**Fresh chat**

A new conversation without the previous thread's context.

**Running job**

One job repeatedly improved through the course.

**Plan-first**

AI shows the planned method and waits for approval.

**Project**

A workspace that holds stable files and instructions for related chats.

**Knowledge**

Files/sources stored for the Project to read.

**Standing instruction**

A reusable rule that applies across the Project.

**Temporary / incognito chat**

A conversation designed not to appear in normal history or contribute to memory, subject to provider retention rules.

**Review thresholds**

Stakes, reversibility, audience and regulatory exposure.

**Delegation Record**

A one-page record of the brief, run, checks and decision.

**Connector**

A connection allowing AI to use information or actions from another app/service.

## 🛠️ Practical Work: Do, Don't Just Read

The course is designed around a single **running job**. Carry one subject through all concepts.

1.  Ask AI a simple question about courses. Then delegate the same need as a job that produces 3 current options in a table. Compare the work left for you.
2.  Run the job once as a step-by-step recipe and once as an outcome. Compare which method found better options.
3.  Write your six-line **OCCADV** Delegation Brief and run it.
4.  Add a constraint requiring current information. Check one source and its date yourself.
5.  Give AI a source. Require source-only answers and evidence. Check one piece of evidence against the original.
6.  Turn the result into a real deliverable. Check that uncertainty/caveats survive the conversion.
7.  Use plan-first. Make AI wait, change one planned step, then approve. Define one decision it may make and one it must ask about.
8.  Save the brief and run it again unchanged. Compare differences. Put repeated context into a Project if available.
9.  Break the job deliberately by removing constraints/authority. Fix the drift with the smallest intervention, then add the lesson back to the brief.
10.  Perform full verification on an important claim: Identify → Trace → Challenge → Decide.
11.  Run the unchanged brief in another assistant/model/run and record differences without declaring a universal winner.
12.  Complete your Delegation Record.

### Six real-world jobs to practice this week

**1\. Purchase**

Compare two real products under your budget. Verify a current price.

**2\. Weekly summary**

Summarize a topic using recent dated sources. Save the brief for repetition.

**3\. Document**

Give a document, require source-only extraction, and verify the evidence.

**4\. Message/letter**

Delegate a draft but explicitly forbid sending. Check facts/promises.

**5\. Seven-day plan**

Delegate a usable study plan with links and constraints.

**6\. Your own subject**

Write a six-line brief from memory, then compare it with your mature brief to see what you forgot.

## 🎯 What You Must Remember for the Exam

**Loop:** DDOIVA = Define → Delegate → Observe → Intervene → Verify → Accept

  

**Brief:** OCCADV = Outcome → Context → Constraints → Authority → Deliverable → Verification

  

**Verify:** ITCD = Identify → Trace → Challenge → Decide

  

**Decision:** ACIR = Accept → Correct → Investigate → Reject

**Final mental model:** You define the job. AI does much of the work. You watch and intervene. You check what matters. You decide whether the result is fit to use. Delegating work never delegates your accountability.
