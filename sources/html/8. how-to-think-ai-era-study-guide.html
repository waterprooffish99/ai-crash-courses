How to Think in the AI Era

A beginner-friendly guide to using AI without giving away your own judgment. The goal is not to avoid AI. The goal is to make AI your thinking partner, while you remain the person who evaluates and decides.

The one ruleThe deliverable is not just the answer. It is the documented evidence of thinking behind the answer.

01 • Core idea

AI should extend your thinking, not replace it

🧲 AI Gravity

AI gravity means the constant pull to outsource more and more of your thinking to AI.

Why it matters: if you always ask AI first, its answer can become your opinion before you have formed your own.

Simple example: You need to choose a course. Instead of first asking “Which course should I take?”, write what you currently think and why. Then use AI to test that position.

💪 Cognitive Capital

Cognitive capital is your built-up ability to reason through problems, detect bad answers, and notice missing information.

Why it matters: this “thinking muscle” weakens if you continually skip the difficult thinking and only accept generated answers.

Simple rule: use AI to stretch a skill, not to skip learning the skill.

Think first→Ask AI→Compare→Check→You decide
Exam idea: The course is not saying “use AI less.” It is teaching a counterweight to AI gravity: keep using AI, but preserve your independent judgment.
02 • Full framework

The 6 disciplines

Each discipline answers a particular AI failure mode. Think of them as six mental safety tools.

1
FOUNDATIONS

Prediction Lock

Meaning: write your own position before AI answers.

Why: AI's confident first answer can anchor your thinking.

Four steps: name the real decision → identify the fact that would settle it → commit to your position and reason → state your confidence and what would change your mind.

Example: “I think Course A is better because it teaches the skill I need. I am 70% sure. If Course B includes a real project with mentorship, I will switch.”

Action line: PREDICT BEFORE YOU PROMPT.
2
FOUNDATIONS

Reasoning Receipt

Meaning: record what you did with each important AI claim and why.

Five labels: ACCEPT keep it; REJECT remove it; MODIFY change it; SURFACED AI found something you had not considered; MISSED you found something AI omitted.

Example: AI says each of four presenters should speak 5 minutes, but the presentation is only 15 minutes. Label: REJECT. Reason: the math does not fit.

Action line: DOCUMENT EVERY DECISION.
3
DETECTION

Error Taxonomy

Meaning: instead of vaguely asking “Is this good?”, scan AI output for six named error types.

ErrorSimple meaning
Factual errorA claim is simply wrong.
Logical gapThe conclusion does not properly follow from the reasoning.
False confidenceAI sounds certain without enough evidence.
Missing contextImportant facts about your situation were ignored.
Fabricated sourceA cited source may not actually exist.
Stale factThe information used to be true but is now outdated.

Beginner trick: ask for the exact source, distrust precise numbers with no source, and mark an unchecked claim as MODIFY rather than ACCEPT.

Action line: PREDICT WHERE ERRORS HIDE.
4
DETECTION

Thinking in Systems

Meaning: do not stop at the first effect. Trace what happens next across all groups affected.

Cascade Map = a simple map of consequences, usually three layers deep.

Loop = when a later consequence circles back and changes, weakens, or strengthens the original decision.

Method: write the decision → list affected groups → ask “and then what?” three times for each → find at least one loop.

Example: replace tutors with AI → struggling students stop asking for help → grades fall → students leave → revenue falls → even less money is available for tutoring.

Action line: CASCADE MAPS & LOOPS.
5
ORIGINATION

First Principles

Meaning: test common advice against the facts of your situation instead of accepting it because everyone repeats it.

Named threshold = the exact condition or number where advice stops working.

Weak: “Group study doesn't always work.”

Strong: “When the group grows beyond five people, participation becomes too uneven for this task.”

Why it matters: AI often gives the common or average answer. Your situation may sit outside the conditions where that advice works.

Action line: FIND THE BOUNDARY.
6
ORIGINATION

Working WITH AI

Meaning: you do the thinking and deciding; AI helps with research and drafting.

Three-path comparison:

① Solo: do it yourself without AI.
② AI-only: accept AI's first version unchanged.
③ Collaborative: use AI, challenge it, and make deliberate human changes.

The test: can you point to specific places where your judgment made the collaborative version better? If collaborative and AI-only look nearly identical, you probably over-accepted.

Action line: OVERRIDE & ITERATE.

04 • Decision scale

Do you need all six every time? No.

DecisionExampleSuggested approach
TrivialWhere to eat, routine replyUse none. Just decide.
Somewhat importantChoose a course, buy a laptopPrediction Lock + Error Taxonomy
Important + deadlineCareer choice, big purchase, project proposalPrediction Lock + Reasoning Receipt + Error Taxonomy + relevant extra disciplines
Very important and reasoning will be judgedThesis defense, major recommendation, interview presentationUse all six disciplines
Common misunderstanding: more process is not always better. The course explicitly warns against using all six disciplines for tiny decisions.
05 • 🛠️ Practical Work

The course gives you 4 real projects

These are not coding projects. You practice on decisions from your real life and keep the reasoning trail.

~15 MIN

🎯 Project 1: Call It Before It Happens

Choose a real decision. Write a Prediction Lock. Then run a premortem: imagine your choice failed six months later and ask AI for three likely reasons. Find one risk you missed and write one safeguard.

Done when: you caught one new failure mode and created a safeguard.

~20 MIN

🧾 Project 2: Audit the Confident Answer

Ask AI a real question. Build a Reasoning Receipt. Then scan the answer with the six error types. Verify at least one source and one current number.

Done when: you catch one unsupported, stale, fabricated, or falsely confident claim and correctly name the error type.

~20 MIN

🔁 Project 3: The Question That Unlocked It

Take a problem you are stuck on. Do not ask AI to solve it. Ask AI for five questions you should answer first, ordered by importance. Identify the question that reframes the problem.

Done when: you can explain why your original question was the wrong or incomplete question.

30–45 MIN

🗂️ Project 4: Decision Dossier

Choose one meaningful real decision and run it through all six disciplines. Keep everything in one file: lock, receipt, error catch, cascade, boundary, and human override.

Done when: someone could read the file and understand why you made the decision.

📋 Decision Dossier checklist
  1. Prediction Lock: real decision + your position + what would flip it.
  2. Reasoning Receipt: label at least three AI claims and explain why.
  3. Error Taxonomy: catch and name at least one error type.
  4. Cascade Map: trace effects across three groups and identify a loop.
  5. First Principles: state one named threshold where common advice breaks.
  6. Three-Path Comparison: compare Solo, AI-only, and Collaborative work; keep the human override.

07 • 🎯 What You Must Remember

The whole course in 7 lines

  1. Do not let AI's first answer become your first thought.
  2. Prediction Lock: decide what you think before prompting.
  3. Reasoning Receipt: label AI claims and write why.
  4. Error Taxonomy: scan for factual error, logical gap, false confidence, missing context, fabricated source, and stale fact.
  5. Thinking in Systems: ask “and then what?” and look for loops.
  6. First Principles: find the exact boundary where common advice stops working.
  7. Working WITH AI: keep human judgment in charge; use AI for research, drafting, and challenge.

Memory sentence: Predict → Document → Detect → Trace → Test → Collaborate.
